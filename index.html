About the workshop

The rapid adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs) is transforming how data is accessed, integrated, queried, and analyzed. By enabling natural language interaction and flexible reasoning over heterogeneous information sources, LLMs are opening new opportunities for data management systems. However, their integration into data-intensive applications also introduces fundamental challenges related to factual correctness, consistency, explainability, provenance, and reliability. These limitations become particularly critical when LLMs interact with structured and evolving data, where generated outputs must comply with explicit schemas, integrity constraints, and domain-specific semantics.

Graph-based representations, including knowledge graphs, property graphs, and graph databases, provide a promising foundation for addressing these challenges. By explicitly modeling entities, relationships, and semantic dependencies, graphs can serve as structured sources of evidence for grounding, reasoning, and validating LLM-generated outputs. Conversely, LLMs can facilitate graph construction, data integration, schema mapping, knowledge graph completion, and natural language querying. This bidirectional relationship creates opportunities for developing hybrid systems that combine the flexibility of LLMs with the structural guarantees and established methodologies of data management technologies.

Topics

We invite submissions that address, but are not limited to, the following themes:

  • Graph-enhanced LLMs for data management and integration
  • Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG)
  • Knowledge graphs and property graphs for LLM grounding
  • LLM-assisted graph construction, completion, and maintenance
  • Natural language querying of graph databases and structured data
  • Scalable graph querying, retrieval, and reasoning with LLMs
  • Data provenance, traceability, and explainability in LLM-based systems
  • Graph-based fact verification and hallucination detection
  • Schema mapping, ontology alignment, and semantic validation
  • Data quality, consistency, and constraint-aware generation
  • Benchmarking and evaluation of graph-enhanced LLM systems
  • Applications of trustworthy graph-enhanced LLMs in data-intensive domains
All submission deadlines are end-of-day in Anywhere on Earth (AoE) time zone.
